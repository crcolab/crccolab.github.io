The island’s links out break every year

At the end of February 2026, 15 international and 10 domestic submarine cables served Taiwan. Over the past four years, cables within 24 nautical miles of Taiwan suffered seven to eight incidents a year on average. A cable repair takes weeks to months, because the repair waits for a ship: about 32 days for an international cable and about 110 days for a domestic cable.

From Cables, landing stations, and power, after a MODA press release of April 7, 2026 and 李又如, Open Culture Foundation, September 8, 2026.

The report defines three disruption levels by their effect, not their cause. The three levels form an escalation ladder: each level includes every effect of the level below it. The report anchors its analysis on 50% Degradation. The disruption ladder

  1. 50% Degradation

    The island loses approximately 50% of its foreign-routed bandwidth, the bandwidth that connects it to the global internet.

    Looks like a brownout (slow, lossy service).

  2. Global Cutoff

    Takes the conditions of 50% Degradation and removes the remaining foreign-routed bandwidth.

    Looks like a hard edge (international traffic stops outright).

  3. Island Partition

    Adds the loss of north–south connectivity inside Taiwan to that total global loss.

    Looks like a split between north and south.
