## Description

Whether drone swarms,  AI targeting systems, cognitive warfare or cyberattacks – contemporary conflict has outgrown physical borders, and the speed at which human ethical cognition can process it, bleeding into an unseen algorithmic realm. This is the new frontline of war, choked by the miasma woven from AI computational power distributed by capital moguls and disinformation intertwined. Here, the binary between victors and victims no longer stands, the distinction has collapsed into tangled complicity.

Violence floods in 24/7 as data and code, slow as a relentless form of torture. It manipulates our everyday sense of safety, fractures the digital environments we live in, while demonstrating the fiction of perpetrator’s power. Our cognitive space thus has been reduced, cornered to a condition of waiting: waiting for the missiles and bullets to fall.

In Taiwanese folk religion, the “wandering daughters (孤娘)” refers to unmarried, childless women who are excluded from patrilineal ancestral shrines. Their lives are erased by patriarchal society. Building shrines for them is a way of making the absolute certain yet unsharable, unspeakable pain into an objection of sentience that can be imagined and worshipped.

Guided by this metaphor, Wandering Daughter’s Dronecraft is making its own path through the Bardo – the zone blurring life and death, the authentic or false. By making ritual, art, civil-defense education, and digital activism, the project makes the pain within the grey zone objectifiable – to project it out into the world so that it can be perceived and altered.

We organize “Non-Visual Civil Defense Workshops” to push back against the violent contraction of our cognitive space; concurrently, our “Bardo Narrative Workshops” deploy post-human pseudo-narratives to disrupt the accepted realities of conflict; our "Anti-Surveillance Fashion Workshops" investigate and design ways for people to evade drone identification.

Every artistic experiment, ethnographic research, technological intervention serve as a restorative act that reverses the unmaking of the world wrought by the simmering slow violence, reweaving the grey ruins  it leaves behind.

At AI for All, our team presents fragments from this ongoing process of making. Just as water lanterns in Taiwanese folk rituals call out spirits lost in violent communal conflicts, the Wandering Daughter now accompanies us in confronting the slow violence that infiltrates our digital habitat. Together, by revealing the invisible spectacles of power operating in the gray zone, we create spaces of equilibrium from which to reimagine and resist them.

## On-Site Devices

### 01｜Tents

**Concealment / Camouflage / Anti-Surveillence**

To avoid being detected by drones, tents serve not only as shelters but also as mobile forms of concealment. They obscure human silhouettes and prevent thermal signatures from being detected by infrared sensor.

### 02｜Meshtastic & Radio

**Decentralized Networks / Real-time Communication**

During wartime, cellular towers and internet infrastructure may be cut off, rendering centralized communication systems obsolete. Meshtastic functions as a decentralized communication node, enabling critical information transmission across networks. Radio combined with antennas, can transmit locations and warnings across vast distances.

### 03｜Computers

**Intelligence / Collaboration / Counter-attack**

Computers can be used to gather intelligence, analyze data, coordinate actions, and learn how to build countermeasures such as drones. They can also become tools for launching digital attacks and information warfare.

Over the past few months, [CRC, the Cyborg Resilience Co-Lab](/en/), has been researching drones and talking about how to evade surveillance. We have played with radio and tested Meshtastic, imagining how people could still reach one another if the network went down one day. We have also run workshops, thinking with people from different backgrounds about how to actually prepare if war or disaster really came.

Then, one day, all of this somehow turned into an art exhibition.

We carried this research, these experiments and these discussions into the New Taipei City Art Museum, where they became *Wandering Daughter’s Dronecraft* (孤娘的無人機藝).

### Why “Wandering Daughters”?

In Taiwanese folk religion, some unmarried, childless women cannot enter the traditional patrilineal system of ancestor worship after death. They become beings left outside the family order, yet they may also be worshipped, because people believe they hold spiritual power.

We wanted to start from that kind of existence and ask a question: as technology keeps identifying and classifying the world, and as war relies more and more on drones and artificial intelligence, which people, which bodies, are easily overlooked or even shut out? And so this rather peculiar “Wandering Daughter temple” came to appear in the museum. ✨✨✨

01｜Tent \| If the drones in the sky are watching us, could we slip out of their sight?

01｜Meshtastic and radio \| If the network goes down, can we still find each other?

03｜Computer \| In a world where information, technology and war are intertwined, how do we gather intelligence, work together, and find ways to respond?

They look like three different things, but behind them lies a shared concern: facing an increasingly unpredictable future, perhaps we need not simply wait for things to happen, and can begin to learn, experiment, and look for other possibilities with the people around us?

For CRC, this exhibition has also been a very special process. The members were originally just a few people from different fields (art x academia x engineering x activism) who wanted to make something together. We never expected to end up placing technology, civil defense, art and Taiwanese folk religion in the same space.

This is probably the resilience we imagine: not necessarily having everything ready, but still being able to find people to work things out with when facing the unknown.
