## Change 3 settings

All of them are free features built into your phone or computer — a few taps and they are working.

**1. Set up offline maps** Google Maps → tap the profile picture in the top right → "Offline maps" → "Select your own map" → download the area around your home, the area around your office, and everywhere else you regularly pass through.

**2. Set cloud files to be available offline** For the slide decks, quotations and contract PDFs you need that afternoon, right-click them on Google Drive／OneDrive／Dropbox or whichever cloud drive you use and choose "Available offline" or "Always keep on this device." **Do it once in the mobile app as well**.

**3. Save music and videos** Download the podcasts / music / videos you want for your commute in advance.

Once you are done, remember to turn off all Wi-Fi + switch on airplane mode, and test that these offline files really work.

## Take 3 kinds of screenshots

Just save them to your photo album — you can look at them without a network.

**1. Phone numbers** Family, school／after-school care, your manager, the clients you need to contact that day. **Plenty of people only have someone's LINE and have no idea what their mobile number is.** Remember to copy them onto a slip of paper for your wallet too — that survives a dead phone battery.

**2. That day's schedule** Meeting times, places, the other party's name and phone number.

**3. Tickets and addresses** For paid high speed rail and Taiwan Railway bookings, screenshot the "booking code (number)" and then collect the paper ticket with the booking holder's ID; event QR codes, the street addresses you are visiting, and the shelters near your home／office (you can look these up in the air-raid shelter section of the National Police Agency, Ministry of the Interior, or download the 「警政服務」 police services app in advance and save the offline map inside the 「消防防災 e 點通」 fire and disaster prevention app).

Note 1: Both the high speed rail and Taiwan Railway companies explicitly prohibit the use of screenshotted ticket QR codes.

Note 2: For rules on screenshotting event QR codes, please follow the terms of each organization/company.

## Make 3 agreements

It doesn't cost a cent — you just have to say it out loud.

**1. To family, elders and friends** "Between 2:30 and 3:00 pm on 8/13, LINE messages may not go through — that's the network throttling drill. If anything comes up I'll call you directly, and you can text me." → This one sentence saves the other person a whole afternoon of anxiety~

**2. To colleagues** "No online meetings during this half hour." Move the 14:30 video call earlier to 14:00, or later to 15:10; if it absolutely has to happen, switch it to a voice-only call or walk over and talk face to face.

**3. To colleagues out in the field and whoever picks up the children** Agree on a "check-in time ＋ meeting point," for example "I'll call you at 15:00; if you can't get through, wait for me in the ground-floor lobby." **Whenever you cannot reach each other at the agreed time, head instead for the fixed meeting point you settled on in advance.**

## 14:20 on the day: a 3-minute wrap-up

- Approvals, reports and attachments you send from your phone: hit send before 14:25 and don't cut it fine.

- If your company systems, VPN or online banking **need push verification from a phone app, log in on your computer beforehand and don't log out during that half hour** (systems that use SMS verification codes are unaffected)

- If you need to place an order, transfer money or upload a tender, handle it on a computer on fixed broadband, or simply avoid this half hour

- Wallet check: **cash ＋ a physical EasyCard／iPASS ＋ a physical credit card**. When the connection is very slow, the payment barcode in a mobile payment app may not open.

- Check that your laptop is on the office Wi-Fi rather than your own phone hotspot

## 14:30–15:00: how to spend these 30 minutes

**Things you can do**

- Connect to the fixed-broadband Wi-Fi at the office or at home and you will barely notice a thing

- If you need someone, call them; if it isn't urgent, text them

- Schedule it as an "offline work block": proofread on paper, write documents, tidy your desktop, walk over and finish a conversation with a colleague

**Things not to do**

- Don't hammer refresh. The more people retry, the slower it feels

- Don't go changing network settings or the APN — it is very easy to forget how to change them back afterwards

- Don't rush to call your telecom's customer service

## After 15:00

If the network doesn't come back on its own: **switch airplane mode on for 10 seconds and then off again**, or simply restart the phone.

Then spend two minutes asking yourself one question: **which thing just now was the most stuck?** Was it not being able to find someone's phone number, not being able to pay, or not being able to open a file? Write it down — because when the network is damaged by a real typhoon or earthquake, nobody announces the time in advance.
